# How Lingestra recognises signs

Everything below runs on landmarks only (MediaPipe pose + hands, `(67, 3)` per frame, NaN = not
detected). No video or image of the signer is stored. The recogniser **always receives un-mirrored
landmarks**; the "flip camera" view option is display-only.

The tables after the benchmark marker line are generated by `python scripts/benchmark_recognition.py`
(it rewrites everything below the marker); the text above the marker is hand-written and kept.

## Why the old version failed on the webcam

The Keras dictionary model was trained on one take of each sign from one dictionary signer and had no
notion of "none of the above" for real input: on a webcam it answered almost only *Aunt*, *Brother*
and *Lactation Brother* (domain gap). It also treated raising and lowering the hands as part of the
sign, and a pause between key poses changed what it saw. The personal matcher replaces it as the
primary path.

## 1. Signing zone: raise and lowering are not part of the sign

`features.ZoneTracker` (streaming) / `features.segment_signing` (offline, same state machine) /
`features.trim_active` (the cut used everywhere).

* **Hand height** per frame = height of the highest wrist above the hip line in units of the
  shoulder-to-hip span (0 = hip, 1 = shoulder). Pose only, so it survives hand drop-outs; references
  are carried across short pose gaps; NaN never raises or lowers the hands.
* **Hysteresis**: enter above `max(0.38, rest + 0.30)`, leave below `max(0.25, rest + 0.15)`; `rest` is
  adapted to where this signer lets the hands hang. Entering needs 0.08 s, leaving 0.2 s (debounce).
  Lowering the hands = end of the sign = **stop gesture** for both the recorder and the recogniser.
* **Trimming**: at the start, frames where the wrist is still travelling up fast (> 0.8 span/s) and
  has not yet reached the height it holds during the first 0.8 s are the *approach* and are cut; at
  the end the same for the *retreat*. Capped at 0.7 s per side. A side is only trimmed if rest was
  actually observed there (a clip that starts with raised hands is left alone). Typical result on the
  dictionary: 2-7 frames cut per side from the zone span (median 4).
* **Holds** (hands nearly still for >= 0.6 s while up) are remembered, see 3.
* Used by: `SignRecorder` (clip = start of the zone to the lowering; the 3-2-1 countdown, failure
  reasons and quality check are unchanged), `Recognizer`, `library.prepare_animation` (playback
  shows the sign without raise/lower; the raw takes stay intact), `labels.clip_stats`, the matcher's
  template preparation, and the benchmark.
* The cut that the *old Keras model* was trained with is kept as `features.trim_for_model` (used by
  `clip_to_tensor` and `scripts/train.py`) so `models/*.keras` keep working unchanged.
* Known limitation: a sign that itself *begins with a fast upward stroke and no settling* cannot be
  told apart from the raise; the cap (0.7 s) and a "keep the whole span if trimming would eat the
  sign" fallback bound the damage.

## 2. Personal matcher (`lingestra/live/matcher.py`)

Recognises signs from the user's **own recordings immediately, without retraining**.

1. **Features** (`features.sign_features`): per frame, in a body frame (origin = shoulder midpoint,
   x axis = shoulder line, unit = shoulder width, so camera shift, zoom and small roll cancel):
   wrist location relative to shoulders and nose (both hands), hand shape (10 finger joints x 3,
   relative to the wrist, scaled by palm size; both hands), orientation (palm normal and hand
   direction), elbows, and a dominant-hand value. Left/right are **not** interchangeable. A missing
   hand is a masked block (not zeros); a hand that is raised in < 25% of the sign is ignored as a
   whole so a resting hand cannot add noise; short detection gaps (<= 0.2 s) are bridged.
2. **Hold collapse**: consecutive frames within `eps` of the run's first frame (in our own frame-cost
   units) are merged, so a 1 s pause between key poses becomes one key pose.
3. **Arc-length resampling**: the key-pose polyline is resampled to 24 points equally spaced in
   feature space (constant "amount of change" per step, not constant time). Speed is gone.
4. **DTW**, vectorised over all templates at once (pure numpy): open begin / open end (the template may
   start within the first 3 and end within the last 3 query points, so leftover transition frames
   are skipped), Sakoe-Chiba band, small penalty on non-diagonal steps, normalised by path length.
   A masked block present in only one frame costs `c_miss`; blocks absent in both are ignored.
5. **Open-set decision**: per-label acceptance distance = `1.35 x` the largest leave-one-out distance
   between the user's own takes of that sign (clipped to 0.30-0.55: a ceiling just above the 0.45 single-take default, because a looser ceiling (0.80) was measured to roughly triple false accepts of un-enrolled signs once a label had 2 takes); a single take uses the default
   0.45 (chosen from the benchmark to keep false accepts of un-enrolled signs at ~5-15% on the
   dictionary while still accepting the similar takes). A sign is accepted only if its distance is
   below its threshold **and** it beats the second-best label by >= 15%. The runner-up competes if it is
   within threshold or within 25% above its own threshold (no hard cliff: a near tie just over the
   line is a `clarify`, not a confident sign). Two close labels -> `clarify` with both; nothing close -> `unknown` (and the
   landmark window goes to the New signs panel). Confidence `1 - distance / (2 x threshold)` (0.5 at
   the threshold, 1 at a perfect match), reduced a little when the margin is thin. The UI
   "confidence" slider scales all thresholds (0.8 = calibrated, higher = stricter).

Cost: ~2-10 ms per decision for 50 signs x 3 takes, made once per finished sign (not per frame).
Enrolment (feature extraction + leave-one-out calibration) is ~1.5 s for 150 takes and is cached per
take.

## 3. Recogniser integration (`lingestra/live/recognizer.py`)

* Episodes: zone entry -> zone exit. Raise/lowering are already cut off.
* Holds inside an episode (hands up, still >= 0.6 s) are *remembered*; when the episode ends the
  whole episode is compared with the pieces between the holds. If the whole is a confident match it
  is one sign (the holds were pauses between key poses, delays are fine); otherwise, if the pieces
  match, they are separate signs (hands stay up between signs). Pieces that match are emitted, ones
  that do not become `unknown`. An episode longer than 8 s is resolved at the next hold.
  Trade-off: signs signed with hands staying up appear when the episode ends, not at each hold.
* The separator gesture (both hands above the head, held 1 s) still ends the sentence: the sign
  before it is classified first, the hands-up pose itself is ignored.
* `Event.source` is `'personal'` or `'model'`. An unanswered clarify question is dropped when the
  signer carries on with the next sign.
* The Keras model is optional and secondary: used when `use_model=True`, or automatically only
  while there are **no** personal templates; it never overrides a confident personal match.
  Worker: `set_use_model()`; default `None` = automatic. For the model the recogniser passes the zone
  span WITH its approach/retreat and the model applies its own training-time cut.
* `RecognitionWorker.set_templates(SignLibrary.templates())` (thread-safe command) and
  `clear_sentence()` are new; `SignLibrary.is_active/set_active/templates` decide what is used
  ("Use in conversation": own signs and signs with a user take are on, dictionary signs off).

## 4. Limitations

* The matcher's single-dictionary-take accuracy is modest (see the cross-take tables): the second
  dictionary take of a sign is often a different performance or variant (about 40% of second takes
  are not even in the top 10 of 101), which no matcher can fix. Enrol 2-3 takes of your own.
* One dictionary signer in the data, no real webcam data in this sandbox: the numbers are a proxy,
  **real webcam validation by the team is still required** (also for the 0.45 default threshold).
* Landmark x/y are normalised by image width/height; the aspect ratio is unknown to the recogniser,
  so a 4:3 webcam and a 16:9 dictionary video distort distances differently (own recordings are
  unaffected because they come from the same camera).
* Signs whose only difference is a subtle finger pattern (many numbers) are close in feature space.
* Two signs separated by a hands-up pause where the first half of one sign looks like a complete
  other sign are ambiguous by construction (the whole episode wins if it matches confidently).

## What the benchmark shows (hand-written summary of the run below; re-read after re-running)

* **Cross-take, dictionary data (the realistic proxy):** with 30 enrolled signs, one dictionary take
  each: nearest sign right (top-1) 44%; answered with the right sign 26%, with a wrong sign 7%, asked
  "A or B?" (right one among them) 3%, rejected as unknown 63%. With 100 signs: top-1 33%; 18% right,
  8% wrong, 6% clarify-ok, 55% unknown. This is modest on purpose: the threshold favours rejecting
  over guessing, and about 40% of second takes are not in the top 10 of 101 (they are a different
  performance / variant of the sign), which no matcher can fix.
* **Invariance, isolated** (same take as template and query, stress on the query): speed 0.5x-2x,
  1-3 pauses of 0.3-1.5 s, raise/lower + idle lead-in/out, camera shift/zoom/rotation and jitter +
  hand drop-outs all stay at 95-100% answered-correctly (pauses are now drifting holds: 95%); everything combined 90%. Compare the cross-take
  rows: stress changes the cross-take numbers by only 0-5 points, but that is a floor set by the
  take-to-take mismatch, so those rows say little about invariance; the same-take table is the evidence.
* **Open set:** un-enrolled signs are falsely accepted 4% (10 signs), 7% (30), 11% (100). More
  enrolled signs = more chances that something is close; the confidence slider tightens it. A review
  found that the old calibrated ceiling (0.80) tripled false accepts with 2 takes per sign (10.9% -> 31%
  on 30 signs); the ceiling is now 0.55 and the benchmark has a 1-take vs 2-take open-set table
  (about 4% vs 8% under every stress). Two takes still double the false accepts, so **more own takes
  buy robustness to your own variation, not better rejection**; this is not measured with real own takes.
  Precision (right / answered) is 81-85% at 30 signs. Wrong answers and false accepts are spread over
  many labels, except that at 100 signs 'Full' and 'Mirror' absorb a visible share, and non-sign motion
  (hands raised and wandering) is falsely accepted ~11% of the time, **always as the same label
  ('Full')**: an attractor, which should be checked on real webcam data. Through the full Recogniser
  (hold splitting) un-enrolled signs, including one with a 1 s drifting pause, produced a sign in
  0-2% of streams.
* **Hold collapse + arc length vs textbook DTW on native frames** (comparator (b)): the accuracy is the
  same within noise (it is sometimes a few points better, sometimes worse; textbook DTW is a little
  better on the hardest combined stress, 92% vs 89% same-take), because DTW's own horizontal steps
  already absorb a held pose. What the collapse + resampling buy is a fixed, small problem size:
  ~9 ms per decision for 150 templates versus ~100x that for textbook DTW (the reason (b) was not run
  for 100 signs), and thresholds that do not depend on how long the signer lingers. Do not read the
  benchmark as proof that collapsing improves accuracy: it does not.
* **Keras model (a):** only two Family signs have a second take, so the cross-take row is anecdotal
  (n = 2). On the take it was trained on it gets 67% clean and 33-42% under speed / pause / transition
  stress (with wrong answers up to 42%), vs 96-100% for the matcher in the same-take table. On a real
  webcam it is worse (domain gap), hence optional.
* Not measured here: real webcam video, other signers, accuracy gain from the user's own multiple takes.

## Continuous signing: several signs in a row without lowering the hands

**The problem (user report).** After signing `ok` and, without lowering the hands, `7000`, the app asked
"did you mean ok or 7000?". Cause: the recogniser only split an episode (hands-arrive to hands-leave) at
HOLDS (hands up and still >= 0.6 s). With continuous motion between two signs there is no hold, so the
WHOLE episode was matched against single-sign templates, neither matched well, and the answer was
'clarify' between the two partial matches. Measured on the benchmark below this is 0% correct sequences.

**What it does now** (`Recognizer.continuous`, default ON, Settings: "I can sign several signs in a row
without lowering my hands"; `continuous=False` is the previous behaviour, bit for bit):

1. `matcher.decode_sequence(episode)` turns ONE episode into a list of `Segment(label, start, end,
   distance, confidence, runner_up, ambiguous, kind)`, `kind` = `sign` | `clarify` | `unknown`.
2. Features are computed once per episode. The hand-used rule and the dominant-hand value depend on the
   stretch they are computed over, so they are decided per window (spotting) or per segment (judging).
3. Hold collapse + arc-length resampling at a FIXED step (0.10 feature units) for the episode and for every
   enrolled take ("dense points": speed and pause invariant). Every dense point is a potential boundary, so
   no separate boundary proposals (speed valleys, hand-shape changes, grids) are needed; speed valleys and
   shape changes are exactly where arc length per frame is small / large.
4. SPOTTING: subsequence DTW of all templates at once (template fully consumed, free start and end inside the
   episode; same frame cost, step set and step penalty as the single-sign DTW; templates processed in 4
   length buckets). Output: for every end point, the best start and normalised distance per template.
5. SEGMENTATION DP over the points: cost = sum(span x distance) + 1.5 per segment (prefers fewer, i.e. the
   single-sign explanation) + 0.75 per point nobody explains (transitions, unknown signs). Candidates must be
   within 1.35 x the label's calibrated threshold and 0.55-1.9 x the template length.
6. Every chosen segment is re-judged by the ordinary open-set matcher (24 points, calibrated per-label
   threshold, 15% margin over its own runner-up): sign / clarify / unknown. Pruning: only templates whose
   label matched some stretch (or the 12 nearest) are compared in full.
7. Single-sign preference: neighbours are merged when their joint span matches as ONE sign at least as
   well as the parts (distance / threshold of the whole <= mean of the parts); the whole episode is tested
   the same way last. The same label twice with no transition (< 0.12 s) is one sign. In a multi-segment
   explanation a sign must match at <= 1.0 x its threshold (<= 0.7 x when part of the episode matches
   nothing: fragments of an un-enrolled sign must not become words). Stretches that match nothing become
   ONE 'unknown' segment only if they are >= 0.6 s (1.0 s at the ends of the episode), not a single straight
   move (hands travelling from one sign to the next) and not the hands travelling to / from rest.

**Streaming** (`Recognizer`): while the hands stay up, the uncommitted tail of the episode is re-decoded every
~0.25 s of new signing (and when a hold starts) with `final=False` (a sign in progress at the open end is not
forced into fragments of other signs). A word is COMMITTED when its segment ended > 0.3 s ago, the previous
decode found the same word at about the same place, something came after it (a next segment, or a hold) and
it matched well enough (distance / threshold <= 0.85, i.e. it passes the open-set test with room to spare; <= 0.25 when only a hold is the evidence, because the
first half of a sign matches its own template at ~0.5, so a pause INSIDE a sign must not commit it). Words
are committed strictly in order and never change; the tail restarts at the next segment. When the episode
ends (hands lowered, separator, hold timeout) the rest is decoded and everything is emitted. A `clarify`
segment is a question for THAT segment only (a placeholder keeps its place in the sentence, `confirm`
replaces it in place, `reject` removes it; a newer question replaces an unanswered one). An `unknown`
segment carries only its own landmark window. `MAX_EPISODE_S` no longer cuts a sign. `source` stays
'personal'. The optional Keras path is consulted only for segments the personal matcher could not decide.

**Benchmark** (`python scripts/benchmark_continuous.py`; 30 enrolled dictionary signs, ONE enrolled take each;
30 sequences per cell and seed, 5 seeds). Sequences join the signing spans of 2-3 different signs with a
0.1-0.4 s interpolated transition, no hold, optional per-sign speed 0.6x/1x/1.6x, 0.2-0.4 s pauses and jitter,
wrapped in raise / lower so the zone tracker sees exactly one episode. Streaming through the real Recogniser.

of them was used for debugging), so numbers quoted from them earlier were NOT held out. The table below is a
single run on fresh seeds 7101-7105 after the last code change; the parameters were not tuned on them (tuning

Speed: `decode_sequence` of an 8 s (200 frames) episode with 4-5 signs, 50 signs x 3 takes (150 templates), query NOT
an enrolled clip, median of 3 per episode: **60 ms mean, 86 ms max** (113 ms max for the old path on the same
load); earlier runs of the same benchmark on a busier machine gave 120-140 ms, so plan for ~60-150 ms per
full decode. The per-frame worst case during streaming was 49-76 ms because decodes run every ~0.25 s on the tail
only. A single sign is ~30 ms. Memory: dense templates are a few MB.

**Targets, stated plainly.** The 80% sequence target is met ONLY for the easy same-clip queries (91% / 85%),
which measure that the decoder cuts a join correctly, not that it recognises an independent performance.
On cross-take data it is NOT met (1% / 0%; 45% / 18% when restricted to signs the matcher knows). Single-sign
false-split <= 5%: met for same-clip (1%), NOT met for cross-take (7%, 11% on the earlier seeds): about 1 in
10 cross-take single signs now gets extra clarify / unknown events with continuous mode on. Whether continuous
mode reaches 80% on the user's own independent examples is untested here; the real test is the webcam.
Cross-take overall stays low for a reason unrelated to continuity: the dictionary's second take of a sign
is a different performance, so single-sign cross-take accuracy is only ~20% and a sequence needs every sign right.
Limits: a single sign slower than `MAX_TAIL_S` (10 s) is cut in two and can be emitted twice; a word that stays
unsure is committed after `STALE_S` (4 s) or when the 10 s cap would drop it (it is emitted as decoded, never
silently dropped). The zone tracker keeps the whole episode's landmarks and rebuilds its span on every decode, so
very long episodes (minutes) cost a little more per frame (1.6 ms/frame at 12 words, ~6 ms at 240).

**How to read this honestly.**

* The dictionary clips were each performed in isolation from rest, so the synthetic joins (straight
  interpolation, 0.1-0.4 s) are a PROXY for real co-articulation ("movement epenthesis"): real hands do
  not travel on a straight line from one end pose to the next start pose, and some real signs change shape
  at their edges when they follow another sign. **The real test is the user's webcam.**
* Cross-take is bounded by single-sign cross-take accuracy (24%), hence the 'matchable' rows: they keep
  the other enrolled signs as distractors but draw the sequence only from signs the matcher answers
  correctly when shown alone. Even there, 3-sign sequences are 25%: every extra sign multiplies the risk.
* With 2-3 of the user's OWN takes per sign (the recommendation), the single-sign accuracy is far higher
  than the dictionary cross-take; this was not measurable here.
* Two failure modes remain: (1) a sign that matches only at ~0.9 x its threshold in isolation has no margin
  once its boundaries are slightly off inside a sequence: it becomes `unknown` (a deletion, never a wrong
  word: substitutions are 0-4%, insertions <= 1%); (2) fragments: an un-enrolled sign whose first part
  looks like an enrolled sign can yield "that sign + unknown" (the un-enrolled false-accept rate rises from
  4% to 8% on this benchmark, the cost of allowing several words per episode; the 0.7 fragment rule is what
  keeps it from being worse). The sensitivity setting ("Fewer mistakes") tightens both.
* Streaming decisions cost accuracy compared with decoding the finished episode offline (about 99% / 97%
  same-clip, 57% / 32% matchable cross-take, measured earlier on seeds 2001-2003 that were also used for debugging): words are only committed when stable and
  confident, otherwise they wait for the end of the episode, which is also when most mistakes of the
  streaming rules are made (a cyclic sign performed longer than its template can still be cut in two).
* The synthetic dictionary joins include the dictionary clips' own idle tails (hands half-lowered for
  seconds), which the decoder reports as an extra 'unknown' stretch; real transitions do not.

# Recognition benchmark

Generated by `python scripts/benchmark_recognition.py` (seed 0). Do not edit by hand.

### Words appear while the hands are still up (tuning note)

User report: while signing several words in a row the words only appeared when the hands were lowered. Cause:
the commit rule above required distance / threshold <= 0.4 for a word followed by another sign. That was tuned
on synthetic joins whose matches are nearly perfect; a real repeat of a sign (second take) rarely gets that
close, so most words waited for the final decode when the hands were lowered. `scripts/measure_commit_timing.py`
measures this (share of words, except the last, that are emitted before the hands start to lower). Held-out
seeds 7101-7105, 30 enrolled signs, 2-3 sign sequences, second take as the query:

| rule | words shown while hands up | sequence exact | single signs wrongly split |
|---|---|---|---|
| old: next-sign bar 0.4 | 52% | 34% | 7% |
| next-sign bar 0.85 (now) | 91% | 33% | 7% |
| next-sign bar 0.85 + 'settle' rule for the last word | 89% | 31% | 10% |
| next-sign bar 0.85 + pause bar 0.5 | (87%) | 37% (3 seeds) | 10% (3 seeds), and a pause INSIDE a sign committed its first half (2 tests failed) |

Kept: the next-sign bar 0.85 (`COMMIT_SURE`). Rejected and measured: a looser pause bar (`HOLD_SURE` stays 0.25: the
first half of a sign matches its own template at ~0.5) and a 'settle' rule that commits the last word after 0.6 s
(`SETTLE_S`, now off: it barely fires, because the hands are lowered right after the last word, and it cost
accuracy). The last word still appears when the hands are lowered (median about 0.5 s after they start to lower).
Differences of a few points are within noise at these sample sizes (60-90 sequences).

### Why a message can take long to appear (measured, `scripts/measure_latency.py`, `scripts/measure_commit_timing.py`)

1. **An unanswered question held the whole message for 10 s.** The sentence is only sent when no question is open, and a
   question lived for 10 s. With the hands lowered no finger answer is possible, so the message simply waited
   (measured: sent 10.6 s after the hands started to lower when a question had been asked, 1.8 s when not). Now: after the
   hands have been down for 1.5 s the question gets a further `QUESTION_GRACE_S` = 2 s, then that word is skipped and the
   rest is sent (about 3.6 s).
2. **Many signs switched on make every check slow.** One re-check of the sentence took 0.1 s with 150 signs on, 0.27 s with
   400 and 0.6-0.85 s with 1,000 (more than the 40 ms between camera frames, on top of the ~30 ms hand tracking costs per
   frame), so frames pile up and later words arrive later. The re-check is now throttled to at most ~1/4 of the time
   (`DECODE_DUTY`): real-time factor with 1,000 signs on 0.78 -> 0.23, frames over 40 ms 58 -> 13. Words are still slower
   with that many signs (decode time itself), so the Talk screen warns when more than 150 signs are on.
3. **One doubtful word delays the words after it.** Words are committed in order, and a doubtful word (question / unknown)
   is released only once a later word is final, which in turn waits behind it (cross-take 6-word sentences: mean lag
   about 2.3 s, up to 6 s, words emitted in bursts). Releasing a doubtful word as soon as it is stable halves the bursts
   and cuts the mean lag by 0.3-0.5 s but produces more questions / unknowns and slightly more word errors
   (`UNSURE_WAITS_FOR_SIGN`), so the old rule is kept. A brief still pause between two words ends the word at once.

4. **Holding the last pose of the last word.** A word followed only by a hold needed a near-perfect match (distance / tolerance
   <= `HOLD_SURE` 0.25); a real repeat of a sign is nearer 0.3-0.9, so it waited for the 4 s `STALE_S` backstop (measured on
   noisy synthetic signs with ratio ~0.33: word written 1.7 s after the hold began with the new rule, previously 4+ s). Now a
   hold that lasts `HOLD_COMMIT_S` (1.6 s, adjustable in Settings: Quick 1.0 / Normal 1.6 / Patient 2.5) ends the word, which is
   then accepted at the same bar as a word followed by another sign (`COMMIT_SURE`). The default stays above the longest
   pause inside a sign that the tests tolerate (1.5 s), so 'Quick' trades that tolerance for speed. Match quality alone
   cannot tell 'end pose held' from 'paused in the middle of a sign' (both measured ~0.86 on real cross-take data).
5. **A repeated or look-alike word when the hands were finally lowered.** After a word was committed during a hold, the
   frozen pose (it can last seconds) stayed in the not-yet-decoded part, and the final check read it as another word
   (`alpha alpha`, or `gamma beta`). Frames of a hold that follows a committed word are now skipped until the hands move.
6. **Tracking cost.** Body tracking takes 11.7 ms and hand tracking 14.3 ms per frame on this machine (26.8 ms together, a
   camera frame arrives every 33-40 ms). They are independent models, so they now run on two cores at once: 24.4 ms ->
   14.6 ms per frame (1.7x, identical landmarks). With only a few signs switched on, decoding costs a few milliseconds, so more
   cores do not make the words appear sooner; the waits above are rules, not computing.

### Finger answers: what counts as a finger (`lingestra/live/fingers.py`)

* A finger is UP when its tip is >= 1.5x as far from the wrist as its knuckle, DOWN when <= 1.2x (dictionary number signs:
  up 1.8-2.0, down 0.7-0.95). The distances are measured in 3D (x and depth scaled by the camera's width / height, so
  `Recognizer.aspect` is set from the source's frame): with flat 2D distances a finger pointing at the camera looks short (the
  index of a '1' tilted 65 degrees toward the camera read 1.10 = 'folded'; 3D still 2.24) and the readings fluctuate far more
  from frame to frame (spread 0.05-0.13 in 3D against up to 0.8 in 2D on the dictionary's own clips). On those clean, frontal
  clips both readings are equally steady (95% of half-second windows correct), so the gain is for tilted / pointing hands.
* A pick is a MAJORITY VOTE over the last 0.8 s: >= 4 frames with a tracked hand, spanning >= 0.5 s, >= 70% of them showing the
  same number. A frame with a raised hand but no clean number votes against; a frame with NO tracked hand abstains (hand
  trackers drop out a lot on a fist with fingers up: a real report had the hand in 39 of 60 frames, and counting those
  dropouts as votes against made a held pose unselectable).
* The count never depends on WHICH finger is up. The same real report showed the raised finger reported as the pinky while
  the index stayed at 0.93 (the tracker takes a fist for the mirror-image hand when the thumb is hidden). So taught poses are
  compared as sorted measurements, and the thumb is measured from the nearer outer knuckle (index or pinky side).
* The THUMB is ignored unless all four fingers are up (then thumb out = 5, folded = 4): a fist very often has its thumb
  resting alongside, and a relaxed fist on the other hand must not add a phantom finger to the number.
* A half-bent finger (between the two) makes the count unclear, EXCEPT when the four fingers split into a tight 'up' group
  and a clear 'down' group (a foreshortened hand: all up-fingers measure alike); a lone half-bent finger is an outlier and gives
  NO number rather than a wrong one. Two hands add up (6-10); a closed fist (0) is not a number.
* Taught poses carry a version (`CAL_VERSION` 3 = measured in 3D); poses saved by an older version are ignored.
* 'Teach me my fingers' learns the signer's own 1-5 (measurements of five fingers; thumb weighted 0.35) and uses the nearest
  taught pose; the plain geometry is the fallback. 'Check what I see' names the finger it cannot read.

## Honest scope

* All data are the UAE Sign Language Dictionary landmarks: **one dictionary signer** (plus a few re-takes). The cross-take test (template = take 1, query = take 2 of the same sign) is the most realistic proxy available; it is NOT a test on a new signer or on your webcam.
* **Real webcam validation by the team is still required** (different camera, lighting, framing, the signer's own style). Numbers below will differ in daily use. Enrolling 2-3 of your own takes per sign is recommended, but this benchmark only verifies that extra takes do NOT loosen rejection (open-set 1-take vs 2-take table); it cannot measure the gain from the user's own takes.
* Pool: 1102 signs with a good-quality clip, 101 with >= 2 good takes. Vocabulary of size V = V random eligible signs, one enrolled template each (take 1), queries = take 2. Sizes with repeats: [10, 30, 100] x 5 random draws (mean).
* Columns: **top-1** = nearest enrolled sign is the right one (ignores rejection); **accepted ok** = answered 'sign' with the right label; **wrong** = answered 'sign' with a WRONG label; **clarify ok** = asked 'did you mean A or B' and the right sign is one of the two; **unknown** = rejected. accepted ok + wrong + clarify (ok or not) + unknown = 100%. **precision** = accepted ok / (accepted ok + wrong): of the words actually written, the share that were right. **absorbing labels** = the labels that received the most wrong answers (summed over draws): a label that dominates is an 'attractor'.
* **How to read the cross-take stress rows:** they barely move (0-5 points) because the difference between two dictionary takes of the SAME sign dominates; they are a floor set by take mismatch, NOT evidence of speed / pause invariance. The evidence for invariance is the same-take table, where that difference is removed.

## Speed

50 signs / 150 templates (takes): enrolment (feature extraction + calibration) 1.50 s, then **8.0 ms mean / 8.8 ms max per decision** (made once per signed sign, not per frame; budget was < 30 ms).
